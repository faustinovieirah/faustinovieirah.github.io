Research

My research is mostly focused on the connection between the host galaxy, its interstellar medium (ISM) and star formation. What sets the structure and properties of the ISM within a galaxy? Do different galactic environments play a direct role in triggering and/or suppressing star formation? How does stellar feedback (i.e., stellar winds, photoionization, supernovae) fit into this picture? Since these phenomena operate on very different timescales, determining which is dominant (and where in the galaxy) has significant implications on the lifetimes of molecular clouds, and consequently the time-frame under which star formation occurs.


High-resolution dust extinction mapping technique

In order to understand the link between galactic environment and star formation, we need observations that can resolve individual star forming regions as well as the disc structure of galaxies. Throughout my PhD, I have developed a dust extinction technique which is able to image the dust (and gas) in nearby spiral galaxies at a parsec-scale resolution, which resulted in my first publication. As a proof-of-concept, I have applied the method to M51 - a nearby, grand-design spiral galaxy - and obtained a gas surface density map of its entire disc with a resolution of 5 pc (or 0.14”), shown below (from Faustino Vieira et al., 2023).

Parsec-scale molecular clouds

Our extinction technique allows for detailed statistical studies of individual clouds, and is therefore a useful tool to study the structure and characteristics of the ISM as a function of galactic environment and dynamics.

We obtained over 13,000 resolved molecular clouds for M51 (Faustino Vieira et al., 2024) and found that the molecular clouds in this galaxy are feeling the effects of the interaction between M51 and its companion, particularly in the spiral arms.

In a follow-up paper (Faustino Vieira et al., 2025), we succesfully apply the technique to 4 more nearby galaxies of various morphologies, finding that barred galaxies show a more pronounced contrast in molecular cloud properties between centre/disc than un-barred counterparts. Despite the small sample size, we also find that adopting a homogenised resolution and binning all clouds within a given environment will only present an averaged, combined effect, which is not representative of the conditions within each galaxy.


All data products (maps and cloud catalogues) are freely available here for M51, and here for NGC628, NGC1566, NGC4321 and NGC4689.
